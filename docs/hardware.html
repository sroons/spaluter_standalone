Hardware

A few parts, one instrument.

Spiluter is built from parts you can buy off the shelf: a Raspberry Pi, a sound card that sits on top of it, a small touchscreen, and a good power supply. Add a MIDI controller and something to listen through, and it is ready for a table or a stage.

Parts list

What you need.

This is the combination Spiluter was built and tuned on. It is the one to copy if you want it to behave like the original.

01
Raspberry Pi 4 Model B2 GB of memory is enough

The computer inside. It runs the sound engine and the touchscreen interface. One of its four processor cores is reserved for audio alone, so touching the screen does not disturb the sound.

Required
02
Blokas pisoundAudio and MIDI card for the Pi

The Pi has no proper audio output of its own. The pisound plugs onto its pin header and adds good stereo output and input on quarter-inch jacks, plus classic five-pin MIDI in and out. This is where your cables go.

Required
03
Touchscreen, 1024 × 600The instrument's front panel

Every screen is laid out for exactly this size, with every control large enough for a fingertip. It is the entire front panel, so a responsive one is worth having.

Required
04
Power supply, 5.1 V 3 AA proper one, used for nothing else

The official Raspberry Pi USB-C supply or an equivalent. See the note below: this is the part that decides whether the instrument sounds clean.

Required
05
microSD cardWith Patchbox OS

The Pi's storage. Patchbox OS is a free system made by the pisound's makers for building audio devices on a Pi; Spiluter installs on top of it and takes over the screen.

Required
06
MIDI controllerUSB or five-pin DIN

Anything that sends notes and control changes: a keyboard, a pad grid, a box of knobs. Spiluter was developed with an Intech Studio Grid controller on USB, and listens for knobs on MIDI channel 1. You can work from the touchscreen alone, but knobs are where it comes alive.

Recommended
07
USB stickFor your own samples

Put audio files on a stick, plug it in, and use them as the particle in place of the built-in waveforms. Presets remember which sample they used.

Optional
08
A clock sourceDrum machine, sequencer, DAW

Anything that sends MIDI clock. With it, the LFOs and the delay lock to your tempo. Without it, they run free.

Optional
5.1V

Do not economise on power

During development the instrument had a constant crackle, like an old record, that no amount of work on the sound engine removed. The cause was the power: the Pi was running from a USB port on a power strip while also feeding the touchscreen and the controller. A dedicated 5.1 V, 3 A supply cured it completely. If the POWER chip at the top of the screen is not green, fix that before you judge the sound.

Putting it together

From parts to playing.

Stack

Press the pisound onto the Pi's pin header and screw it down. Connect the touchscreen.

Prepare the card

Write Patchbox OS to the microSD card and start the Pi once to finish its setup.

Install Spiluter

Install the Spiluter package for Raspberry Pi. It sets up the audio system for low delay and steady timing by itself.

Plug in and play

Connect your controller and your speakers, then switch on. The instrument is ready when the status chips turn green.

What to expect

Small box, steady hands.

A Raspberry Pi is a modest computer, and a lot of the work on Spiluter went into making it behave like an instrument. With the default sound the engine uses about a tenth of the processor core set aside for it, the audio output runs about a fiftieth of a second behind your hands, and a five-minute stretch of heavy playing measured no audio dropouts.

The DSP reading in the top right of every screen shows how hard the engine is working. Four voices with three formants each is the heaviest setting.

No Pi?

It runs on a laptop too.

The same instrument runs as an ordinary application on macOS, Windows and Linux, using whatever audio interface and MIDI controller you already own. It needs SuperCollider, a free audio engine, and tries to install it for you on first start.

That is the quickest way to find out whether pulsar synthesis is for you before you buy a single part.
